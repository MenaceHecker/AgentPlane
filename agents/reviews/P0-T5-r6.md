## Security review

- **Spawn ordering:** the ownership record/log is reserved before fork. After success, PID adoption and stderr attachment precede the fallible stdin close. The whole post-fork block enters common recovery on failure. If adoption fails, recovery attaches the PID through the existing normal paths; separate stderr is attached before the exception leaves. The primary exception is raised, not replaced by a successful cleanup result.
- **Descriptor ownership:** adapter failure means no child under the existing POSIX adapter contract; reservation/captures are forgotten/closed and stdin released. Adoption failure retains the child/captures for reports, attempts bounded recovery and releases the still-untried stdin duplicate. Close failure transfers the duplicate to a local variable and clears in_fd BEFORE attempting close; finally cannot accidentally retry a potentially closed/reused integer. Stdout/stderr remain attached for diagnostics and Owner.close. The tested close fault closes first and raises afterward. If a kernel close error leaves its descriptor state ambiguous, portable code cannot promise both no retry and guaranteed closure; the explicit no-retry policy favors identity safety and process-exit descriptor reclamation. This is not a claim of zero open fds under every kernel/close failure.
- **Recovery:** _emergency_reap remains the same bounded normal poll/signal/poll implementation. It marks collection REAPED before decoding, polls immediately before KILL, refuses signals on REAPED/LOST, and records LOST/reason on wait/kill/expired recovery. No external PID, ps or process-group fallback was introduced.
- **Gates/invariants:** NEW is not signalable and NEW/LOST never satisfy all-REAPED workdir removal. Sole waiter, SIGCHLD default, flag-only INT/TERM/HUP, fork-to-registration masking, per-child absolute deadlines and context restoration are unchanged. Retained-directory/manifest/decoy checks are unchanged. The narrow try/finally in kind closes caller duplicates without confusing them with Workdir's held references.
- **Limits:** prior direct-child-only, supervisor-death, uncollectible-kernel-state, responsive-owner-IO and hostile-same-user filesystem limits remain. No broader fd/container/tenant-isolation or unconditional wall-clock guarantee is claimed.
